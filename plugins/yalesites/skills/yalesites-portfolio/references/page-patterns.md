# Page-building patterns (2026-10: 296 sites, 48,285 pages)

**How to read the tags.** *Common* = what the crawl shows most sites do (frequency only). *Recommended* = frequency, reviewer judgment on screenshots, and the team's layout conventions agree. Common is not endorsement.

## Page length

- Blocks per page: median 2, 75th percentile 6, 90th percentile 13, max 816.
- Long pages (12+ blocks): 2,570. Wayfinding blocks present on them: Accordion 46.0%, Divider 41.5%, Quick Links 16.0%, Custom Cards 12.3%, in_this_section 7.4%, Link Grid 5.4%, Tabs 2.8%.

## Homepage composition (reviewer findings on 24 block-rich homepages)

**Exemplar homepages:**
- **hospitality** — https://hospitality.yale.edu: Strong alternation of full-bleed color bands (blue, gold, blue) with white image-led sections gives a clear rhythm over a long page. Orientation hero, then a task-oriented link grid (menus, dining, plans, order), then proof/events, then conversational feedback and an app/newsletter close. Every band has one job and the page ends on a useful action trio plus a dark footer.
- **peabody** — https://peabody.yale.edu: Clear visitor narrative: hero with a plan-your-visit link, a short intro with the free-admission hook, a featured-exhibition band, then Explore, What's Happening and Stories sections. Each section has its own heading, and dividers plus generous whitespace set the pace. It ends with paired buttons and a useful footer with hours, instead of trailing text.
- **ipch** — https://ipch.yale.edu: Classic and complete: hero, one-line mission statement on a tinted band, a spotlight with CTAs, four program cards, a LinkedIn callout, news, then social/newsletter and a closing band. Light/dark blue banding is consistent and pacing is even.
- **planetarysolutions** — https://planetarysolutions.yale.edu: Strong rhythm of light and dark: a hero, then video beside a mission statement, then a navy band of four paired action blocks. Reports, news and a quote band follow, then a newsletter CTA, a filterable story grid and an ending. The navy bands and the full-width quote punctuate a long page so it never feels like a text wall.
- **onha** — https://onha.yale.edu: Three stacked fact bands (green, pale blue, navy) each with a heading and three stats make the case for the office with proof, not prose. Color changes signal topic changes, and the story banner then events turn proof into action.
- **global-nursing** — https://global.nursing.yale.edu: A compact page where every block earns its place: a big image banner, a mission statement, a navy facts band with proof numbers, news, a report and a closing LinkedIn CTA. The only dark band mid-page is the stats, which gives it weight, and the page is short and scannable.
- **divinity** — https://divinity.yale.edu: Alternates large full-width photographs with short headed text blocks, so the page reads as a story with a clear mission voice. Includes a gray values band, a testimonial, and ends with a two-column Get Started / Connect action band.
- **westcampus** — https://westcampus.yale.edu: Orients first (hero, Our Campus, then quick links) and then gives events and news. It alternates white, gray and navy bands and ends with two CTA bands. The empty gap before the footer is a flaw.

**Composition patterns seen:**
- *Color-band chapters*: Alternating full-width theme bands (navy, tint, gold, gray) so each section reads as a chapter. (e.g. hospitality, onha, ipch)
- *Hero then task tiles*: A hero followed immediately by 3-4 tiles/cards for the main visitor tasks. (e.g. hospitality, ipch, mcdb)
- *Stat bands as proof*: Fact blocks grouped under a heading on a colored band to give evidence at a glance. (e.g. onha, yalesites)
- *Feed-driven lower half*: News and events views after the narrative, usually with a View all button. (e.g. jewishstudies, cultivatingconversation, climateandhealth)
- *Alternating image/text spotlights*: Content spotlights with image left then right to explain programs. (e.g. mcdb, jewishstudies)
- *Closing action band*: Final colored band with subscribe, apply or contact buttons just above the footer. (e.g. ipch, divinity, jewishstudies)
- *Hero, orient, cards, news, CTA band*: Hero, a short mission or intro, a row of destination cards, then news or events, then a banded CTA before the footer. (e.g. peabody, westcampus, planetarysolutions)
- *Navy band as chapter break*: Full-width dark color section that groups paired headings and buttons or stats, to break up long white pages. (e.g. planetarysolutions, global-nursing, registrar)
- *Stat band as proof*: A facts block with 3-4 numbers gives quick credibility right after the intro. (e.g. global-nursing)
- *Alternating image/text spotlights*: Large image with an overlapping colored text box, repeated to present 3-5 programs. (e.g. wff, ypsa, peabody)
- *Quote band as pacing device*: A full-width quote placed between content groups gives a visual rest and adds voice. (e.g. planetarysolutions, ypsa)
- *Service-desk layout*: Hero, services cards, quick links band, FAQ accordion, and a hours/contact band. It is task-focused and has little imagery. (e.g. registrar, enterprisemonitoring, usability)

**Length:** Roughly 3,500 to 7,000 px (about 9 to 14 sections) works when each section has a distinct job and color change; hospitality (6,800 px) holds attention through bands, while mcdb and ipch (4,300 to 5,800 px) feel complete. Pages become too long or slack when text or feeds stack without bands (cultivatingconversation at 8,900 px with empty gaps, divinity at 9,200 px relying on huge photos). Event microsites like tinsleyworkshop can be text-heavy only if a sidebar and clear actions are present. Pages of 9-15 blocks at 3,300-5,500px work best (Global Nursing, West Campus, Peabody, Registrar). Pages that exceed about 7,000px (Poorvu 10,700px, Carbon 10,000px, YPSA 9,700px) need clear chapter breaks and a distinct priority CTA, otherwise they flatten. Single-purpose service sites can stay short at about 3,300px if every block is a task link.

**Endings:** Strong pages end on a purposeful action band just above the footer: subscribe/newsletter, apply and contact side by side, or four nav buttons (ipch, divinity, hospitality, jewishstudies). Weak ones trail off with a news feed, an empty gap, a map or acknowledgments. The strongest pages end on one clear CTA band (follow, subscribe, apply, request) or a View All button with a footer that has contact and hours. Weak pages end in a bare form, a calendar, a run of text blocks, or several stacked CTA bands. Avoid empty white gaps before the footer.

**Anti-patterns:**
- climateandhealth: Large blank gap (several hundred px) under the intro text, apparently from stacked dividers/empty blocks, reads as a broken page; the XToll feature is faded and low-contrast.
- cultivatingconversation: Huge empty void before the closing section, a stray gap in the Conversation Tip area, and a 'What Is Cultivating Conversation?' explainer buried at the bottom instead of near the top. Several dividers stacked produce dead space.
- tinsleyworkshop: A single text-heavy event page: long rationale prose plus sidebar lists with no visual breaks beyond one photo. Fine for a workshop microsite but reads as a document, and ends on a map and acknowledgments rather than a registration action.
- commencement: Three near-identical image+navy callouts in a row (News, Know Before You Go, Ask a Question) are repetitive and heavy, with the key dates sidebar left beside empty space; ends with Contact and an accessibility band rather than a strong action.
- yalesites: Sections vary widely in density and many different treatments (gray, navy photo, white) compete; the migration pitch is deep in the page and the page ends with two similar gray help bands plus a long footer.
- jewishstudies: Dense opening text before any navigation to tasks; events list of three large stacked items makes the lower half long and repetitive.
- your: Large navy news band dominates the top; More to Explore cards have uneven heights and a block of empty space precedes the footer; no closing action.
- mcdb: Pleasant but ends on a plain news feed and More News button with white space; hero text over busy image is hard to read.
- carbon-elti: A single-purpose course page is built as a very long stack of text blocks and a 9-person portrait list; no repeated CTA mid-page, and the Apply action is buried below the people sections.
- urbanstudies: A huge dark empty video banner area pushes content down, and the page ends on a bare webform and a mostly empty calendar of 'multi-day event' placeholders, so the ending is weak and the calendar adds noise.

## Page-building guidance

Merged reviewer guidance, deduplicated and tagged against the crawl data and the team's layout conventions (end on a non-text block; pad adjacent Text blocks).

- **[recommended]** Open with one hero, then give visitors their top 3-4 tasks as tiles, quick links or a link grid before any prose. *(Reviewers agree; the crawl shows Quick Links and Custom Cards are the two most common blocks directly after a Grand Hero.)*
- **[recommended]** Put a 2-4 sentence orientation block (what this is, who it is for) right after the hero, not at the bottom of the page. *(Reviewers flagged buried explainers as a top homepage failure; Grand Hero followed by Text is the most common opening pair.)*
- **[recommended, rare in practice]** Put proof within the first two screens: a Facts and Figures band or one attributed quote, not paragraphs. *(Reviewers' strongest pages all do this, but Facts and Figures is used on only 12.5% of sites.)*
- **[recommended, uncommon]** Change the background color or theme between sections so each reads as a chapter; avoid more than two adjacent white sections. *(Every exemplar homepage alternates bands; most sites leave blocks on the default theme (component-theme one on 84% of grand heroes).)*
- **[recommended]** Give every section one heading, one short blurb and one button; never stack three or more identical callouts or CTA bands. *(Reviewers called out repeated callouts and stacked CTAs on four of 24 homepages.)*
- **[recommended]** Place news and events feeds in the lower middle of the page, limited to three items with a View all button, and do not let the page end on a feed. *(Ending on a View is the second most common page closer (12.6% of built pages); reviewers rated those endings weak.)*
- **[recommended; the common pattern is the opposite]** End on a colored action band (subscribe, apply, contact, next step), never on plain text, a map or a bare form. *(Text closes 65% of built pages. This is the single biggest gap between what is common and what reviewers and the team's end-on-a-non-text-block convention recommend.)*
- **[recommended]** Never use stacked Dividers or empty blocks for spacing; check the rendered page logged out for blank gaps before the footer. *(Blank-gap pages were the most frequent compositional defect reviewers found.)*
- **[recommended]** Cap a homepage at about 15 blocks and link out to deeper content; on any page past 12 blocks, add wayfinding (Accordion, Dividers with headings, Quick Links, In This Section). *(Long pages (12+ blocks) use Accordion on 46% and Divider on 41%; the strongest long pages use color bands as chapters.)*
- **[recommended]** Keep Text blocks short and pair them with an image, card row or spotlight; avoid five or more consecutive Text blocks. *(Text is 99% adopted with 78k instances; reviewers' weakest pages were text stacks.)*
- **[common, not recommended]** Opening a page with a plain Text block and closing it with Text. *(Text opens 26% and closes 65% of built pages; use a hero or banner to open and an action band to close instead.)*
